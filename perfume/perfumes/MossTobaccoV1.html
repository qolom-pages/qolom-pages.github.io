Moss Tobacco

Moss Covered Tobacco Leaves

Initially part of a theme I dubbed "grandeur" to signify the extensive ratio of oud present in the perfume, I've dropped the grandiose and brought this composition to it's origin.

A quiet and complex base-heavy composition that focuses on the oakmoss (unfortunately IFRA complaint) and it's co-mingling with a warm damp tobacco leaves.

The oud matrix used here is 38% - a mixture of clean Trat and velvety dark Burmese, an interesting blend that gives an alluring enigmatic character to an otherwise simple Trat oil.

Originally, Grandeur was a dense tobacco gourmand perfume that had plenty of red roses, after careful deliberation the blend was separated into three distinct blends.

The first of the three is Moss Tobacco, a play on typical chypre oud perfumes, the blend opens sparking with pink pepper and cedrat top note (as suggested by Fizal from Fizal Privee).

The bridge between the top and the velvety Burmese is a jasmine absolute that helps keep the physical density of the composition alongside it's introduction of indolic softness.

As many know, no one vetiver is the same, using two distinct distillations of vetiver allows supporting the moss and tobacco leaves without overcrowding and overpowering either one material.

The reticent personality of this Moss Tobacco slowly leaves with the speedy diffusion using a dilution of myrrh, vanilla, and violet extracts.

Perfume Architecture

Top Cedrat
Top-Heart Pink Pepper
Heart Jasmine, White Rose, Violet Leaf
Base-Heart Tobacco, Vetiver, Khus, Myrrh, Cistus, Vanilla
Base Oud Trat x Burmese, Oakmoss, Benzoin
